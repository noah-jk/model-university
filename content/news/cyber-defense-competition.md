A team of eight Cascadia State students placed second out of 22 schools at this summer's regional collegiate cyber defense competition.

Teams were handed a simulated hospital network with weak passwords, unpatched servers, and a stream of urgent requests from "staff," then defended it for 36 hours against a red team of professional security testers. Judges scored teams on keeping services running, responding to incidents, and writing clear reports for nontechnical managers.

"Reports are where a lot of teams lose points," said Professor Quinn Ibarra, the team's faculty advisor. "Our students practice explaining a breach to someone who isn't technical, because that's the job."

The team meets weekly during the academic year and is open to students in any major. Several members have since landed internships with regional health systems and utilities.
