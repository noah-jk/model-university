Senior nursing students will spend part of their clinical rotation this year on a mobile clinic that travels to rural communities in eastern Pierce County, where the nearest hospital can be more than an hour away.

The clinic offers checkups, vaccinations, blood pressure screenings, and follow-up care for chronic conditions. Students work alongside registered nurses and nurse practitioners from the partner health system, and faculty supervise every shift.

"Rural nursing asks you to be ready for anything," said Professor Joaquin Fernandes, who chairs the Department of Nursing. "Our students see a wider range of patients in a semester on the clinic than many nurses see in their first year."

The rotation counts toward clinical hours for the Nursing BS. Graduate students in the MSN program help coordinate care plans and track health outcomes across visits.

Fernandes will talk about the clinic and the future of rural care at a public lecture on April 7.
