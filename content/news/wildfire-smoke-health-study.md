Neighborhoods with community clean-air rooms saw fewer clinic visits for breathing problems during last summer's wildfire smoke, according to a new study led by Professor Chen Abebe.

Clean-air rooms are libraries, community centers, and other public spaces with filtered air, opened to the public on smoky days. The study compared clinic records from neighborhoods with and without nearby rooms during two weeks of heavy smoke.

"The effect was strongest for older adults and people without air conditioning at home," Abebe said. "That tells us where these rooms matter most."

Master of Public Health students collected and analyzed much of the data as part of a community health course taught by Associate Professor Bao Fernandes. Abebe will discuss the findings at a public lecture on January 13.
