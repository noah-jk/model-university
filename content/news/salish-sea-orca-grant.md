A team led by Professor Hana Brennan has received a three-year federal grant to study how shifts in Chinook salmon runs are affecting the Salish Sea's southern resident orcas.

The project pairs underwater acoustic monitoring with genetic analysis of water samples, which can reveal which fish are present without catching them. Researchers will compare where salmon are found with where orcas hunt across four seasons.

"Orcas are telling us something about the whole ecosystem," Brennan said. "If we can understand what they're finding to eat, and where, we can make better decisions about rivers, fisheries, and shipping lanes."

The grant funds two graduate research assistantships and summer field positions for six undergraduates, who will spend time on the university's research vessel and in the genetics lab. Associate Professor Tomas Fong will lead the acoustic monitoring work.

Students interested in field positions for summer 2027 should contact the Department of Marine Biology. Brennan will discuss early findings at a free public lecture, "Orcas, Salmon, and a Changing Salish Sea," on October 14.
