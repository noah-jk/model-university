The College of Education and three South Sound school districts have launched a paid teacher residency. Candidates spend a full school year co-teaching with an experienced mentor instead of the usual single-term placement.

Residents receive a stipend from their district and commit to teaching there for at least two years after earning certification. The first cohort of 24 residents started in classrooms this month, in elementary and special education.

"A full year means you see how a classroom is built in September and what it becomes by June," said Professor Reuben Wallace, who chairs the Department of Elementary Education. "That's hard to learn in ten weeks."

The residency is open to students in the Elementary Education BS and the Master of Education. Applications for the 2027–28 cohort open in January.
