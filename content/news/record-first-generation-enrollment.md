Cascadia State welcomed its most first-generation class yet this fall: 43 percent of new students are the first in their families to attend a four-year college.

Admissions staff point to expanded outreach in community colleges and high schools across the South Sound, along with simpler financial aid messaging that shows families their expected cost before they apply.

The First-Generation Student Center has added peer mentors to keep pace. Every first-generation student is matched with a mentor in their first term, and the center hosts weekly study sessions and a monthly family night.

"Being first doesn't mean being alone," said the center's director, Selam Nakamura. "We want every student to know where to go when something doesn't make sense, whether it's a financial aid letter or how office hours work."

Transfer students made up nearly a third of new enrollment, and the number of students in fully online programs grew for the third year in a row.
