A new field course in the Department of Environmental Science sends students to restored rivers in the South Sound to measure how ecosystems recover after dams and dikes come out.

Students sample water quality, count juvenile salmon, and map how gravel and sediment move downstream. Their data adds to long-term records kept by tribal and state natural resource agencies, which partner on the course.

"Restoration is a decades-long experiment," said Professor Eli Brennan, who chairs the department. "Students who take this course are adding a few more data points to a story that started before they were born and will continue after they graduate."

The course is open to students in the Environmental Science BS and minor. Brennan will present findings from the river program at a public lecture on February 24.
